## Why these two classes

[C28.1](../C28-Class-Catalogue/01-the-class-map-by-subsystem.md) surfaced a cluster of ~78 named methods
across `CGarages`, `CReplay`, `CShopping`, `CGangWars`, `CEntryExitManager` and `CPickups` that no chapter
documented. Surveying their disassembly, two of them stand apart: `CPickups` and `CGarages` each own a
**flat, fixed-size array of same-shaped records** whose bounds are written directly into their own methods —
the cleanest kind of structure to recover, and the same shape as C28's `CRunningScript` pool and C2's
streaming-info array. The other four are dominated by loose state-flag globals (a gang-war has booleans, a
replay has buffers) with far less regular per-element layout, so they are left as catalogued leads.

Both pools land on a number that will be familiar to anyone who has modded the game — **620 pickups**, **50
garages** — but nothing here is taken from that knowledge: each count is `(end − base) / stride` with all
three terms read from an instruction, closing with no residue, exactly the arithmetic-that-closes standard
the project uses for a file-format record width. That the independently-recovered numbers match the known
engine limits is the confirmation, not the source.

The two pools also each expose a small **state machine** — a pickup's in-use byte and reuse counter, a
garage's door state — which is where the gameplay actually lives, and which C29.1 and C29.2 read out of the
methods that transition them.
