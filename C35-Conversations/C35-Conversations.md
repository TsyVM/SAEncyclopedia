## Why this chapter

Ped conversations are the small texture of San Andreas's world — two pedestrians trading lines, a gang
member responding to the player. Mechanically it is a tiny state machine: a set of peds currently *in* a
conversation, a *tree* of question/answer nodes a conversation can be at, and a *pool* of the individual
lines. `CConversations` owns all three as fixed-size static arrays, and — like [C34](../C34-Vehicle-Recording/C34-Vehicle-Recording.md)'s
recording system — they are laid out consecutively in memory, so each array's size is pinned by the address
of the next. Here the chain is three links long: the per-ped table ends exactly where the node table begins,
and the node table ends exactly where the line array begins. Three arrays, two boundary equalities, both
closing to the byte.

The chapter also connects two subsystems the encyclopedia already decoded: a conversation node stores
**text keys**, not text, so the words come from [C19](../C19-GXT-Text/C19-GXT-Text.md)'s GXT (resolved by
the same key-hash), and the spoken audio comes from [C20](../C20-Audio/C20-Audio.md)'s speech banks —
`CConversations` is the state machine that sequences references into both.
