# Chapter 34 — CVehicleRecording: Recorded-Vehicle Playback

> **Goal of this chapter:** decode the system that plays back pre-recorded vehicle paths — the machinery
> behind the game's "follow this exact route" set-pieces (the train, scripted convoys, driving-school
> ghosts). It has two runtime structures, and both are proven by the cleanest argument in the project: an
> array whose end lands **exactly** on the next global. The recording-file table is **475 × 16 bytes** and
> ends precisely at its own live-count global; the playback state is **16 slots** whose car-pointer array
> ends precisely at the recording table's base.

## Why this chapter, and the two recording systems

San Andreas has *two* unrelated "recording" systems, and it is worth separating them at the outset because
their names invite confusion. [C30.2](../C30-Gameplay-Managers/02-creplay.md)'s **`CReplay`** is the
free-camera *replay* feature — it snapshots the whole world into an 800 KB ring buffer so the player can
rewind and watch. **`CVehicleRecording`**, this chapter, is a different thing entirely: it loads authored
`.rrr` path files from the IMG archives and drives specific vehicles along them, frame by frame, for
scripted sequences. One records *everything* transiently; the other plays back *one vehicle's* authored path
deterministically. They share only the word "record."

`CVehicleRecording`'s appeal for this chapter is methodological: both of its structures are proven by the
project's strongest size argument, the one [C2](../C2-CStreaming/C2-CStreaming.md) used for the streaming-info
array — an array is exactly as long as the distance to the next referenced global. Here it happens **twice**,
and the two boundaries even chain: the playback car-pointer array ends where the recording table begins, and
the recording table ends where its count global begins, so three consecutive globals pack with no gap. That
is arithmetic closing to the byte, and it fixes both the 475 and the 16 without a single assumption.
